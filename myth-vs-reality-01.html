Myth: "Incognito Mode Makes Me Anonymous"

The dark window, the little spy icon, the word "Incognito". It is easy to see why so many people believe private browsing makes them invisible. It does not. It makes you invisible to one audience only: the next person who uses your computer.

The myth "If I use incognito or private browsing, websites cannot track me, my employer cannot see what I do and nobody knows who I am."

The Reality: It Is About Your Device, Not the Internet

Every major browser has a private mode: Incognito in Chrome, InPrivate in Edge, Private Browsing in Firefox and Safari. They all do essentially the same thing, and Google's own help page for Chrome is clear about it.

What incognito does. When you close the incognito window, Chrome does not keep a record of the sites you visited, and it clears the cookies and site data from that session. It also does not sign you in to your accounts automatically.

What incognito does not do. Google's help page says the websites you visit, and your school, employer or internet provider, may still be able to see your activity. Websites that use Google services can also share information about your activity on their sites with Google. And if you sign in to an account while in incognito, that site knows exactly who you are.

What it keeps. Files you download and bookmarks you save stay on your device after the incognito window closes.

Incognito hides your browsing from people who share your computer. It does not hide you from the internet.

Who Can Still See What You Do

  • The websites you visit. They still see your IP address, your browser and device details, and everything you do on their pages.
  • Your employer or school, if you are on their network or using a device they manage. Work devices often have monitoring software that sees everything regardless of browser mode.
  • Your internet provider, which can see which sites you connect to.
  • Advertising and analytics companies embedded in websites, which can recognise your device using techniques that do not rely on stored cookies.
  • Any account you sign in to. Sign in to Google, Facebook or your bank in incognito and they know it is you.
  • Malware on your device. Private browsing offers no protection against keyloggers, infostealers or anything else already running on your computer.

What a Lawsuit Revealed

In 2020, a class action was filed in California alleging that Google tracked people's browsing even when they used Incognito mode, without them realising. In April 2024, court documents set out the terms of a settlement.

Under the settlement, Google agreed to delete or de-identify billions of private browsing records, to rewrite its disclosures to say that it collects private browsing data, including on the Incognito start page, and to block third-party cookies by default in Incognito for five years. The plaintiffs valued the settlement at $5 billion. It did not include damages, and individuals remained free to bring their own claims. A Google spokesperson said the company never associates data with users when they use Incognito mode.

Whatever the legal arguments, the case made one thing very public: incognito mode was never designed to stop the websites and services you use from collecting data.

When Incognito Is Genuinely Useful

  • On a shared or borrowed computer, so your history, cookies and sign-ins are not left behind.
  • Signing in to a second account without signing out of your main one.
  • Checking what a page looks like to someone who is not signed in.
  • Avoiding awkward history, such as researching a surprise present on a family laptop.

If You Actually Want More Privacy

  • Block third-party trackers using your browser's built-in tracking protection, set to its strictest level, or a reputable content blocker.
  • Sign out of accounts you do not need while browsing, and use separate browser profiles for work and personal use.
  • Review your account activity settings, such as Google's web and app activity, and delete or limit what is stored.
  • Use a reputable VPN on networks you do not trust, understanding its limits. We look at those in a later episode of this series.
  • For genuine anonymity, tools such as the Tor Browser are designed for it, with trade-offs in speed and convenience.
  • On a work device, assume your employer can see everything, whatever mode you use.
Verdict: Myth Incognito mode keeps your browsing off your own device. It does not make you anonymous to websites, advertisers, your employer, your internet provider or the accounts you sign in to.

Final Thoughts

Private browsing is a privacy tool for your device, not a cloak for your identity. Use it for what it is good at, and use tracking protection, careful sign-in habits and the right tools when you need more.

Incognito is a closed door to your family, not a disguise on the internet.
